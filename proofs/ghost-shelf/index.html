The ghost shelf

Shoppers click an ad, land on "out of stock", and leave annoyed. The retailer still pays for the click. The idea: an ad that tells you the truth before you tap.

The insight

Shopping ads usually learn about stock from a feed that refreshes about once a day. Between a product selling out and the feed catching up, the ad keeps running. In that gap, money buys disappointment.

A study of 500 retailers found 97% kept paying for clicks on unavailable products, often for 24 to 48 hours.

Source: ShoppingIQ study of 500 global retailers, reported by Search Engine Land. ShoppingIQ sells a fix for this problem. I haven't seen the method, so treat the 97% as a vendor figure, not a neutral one.

Off air at 11:04.

An ad that stops selling the minute the shelf is empty, and says so: "Sold out. Back Thursday. We won't waste your tap."

Why it's a campaign, not a plug-in

  • The fix (faster stock feeds) belongs to retail ops. The story belongs to the brand: a retailer that would rather not sell you a ghost.
  • Honesty about stock is a trust story. "Sold out, back Thursday" earns the next click instead of burning this one.
  • Extensions: real-time "last 3 in your size" creative when stock is low, and a "ghost counter" stunt showing the clicks a brand chose not to buy.
